The Seeker Documentation

Open Brand

The Seeker is an open-source brand: a shared sci-fi universe that anyone can play in, build on, and take in their own direction. The game in this repository, headed for a Steam release, is the trunk of the tree. The branches can be anything: other games, art projects, 3D worlds, music, prints, merch.

The brand isn't owned by a company, or even by us. Its code, art, audio, and lore are open to everyone. Ownership is cultural, and it lives in our collectibles. Holders are the game masters of this shared world. Think of the Gamemakers in The Hunger Games, reshaping the arena while everyone else plays, minus the fight to the death. Holders help decide how the planet changes, which guardians watch over it, and where its story goes next. Anyone can play without owning anything; holders get to shape the world everyone plays in.

You don't need to know how to code, and your version doesn't have to be a 2D pixel-art game. Give the source, assets, and lore to an AI agent and ask for whatever you have in mind: a 3D world, a card game, an animated short, a figure to 3D-print. The repository is already set up for agents (AGENTS.md maps the code). Coming soon is a SKILL.md that teaches any agent the vision, art style, and atmosphere in one go, along with the code that connects the game to the collectibles.

Why open source

AI makes it possible to rebuild almost any product from a screenshot and a prompt. Copyright still exists, but when anyone can recreate a game in an afternoon, a creative project can't depend on it anymore. We saw this coming, and The Seeker has been open source from day one.

So instead of fighting copies, we invite them. Open source spreads on its own: every fork, remix, and spin-off brings The Seeker's world to new people, and the best ideas can flow back into the original.

Owning a piece of the brand

Open source generates attention and culture, but on its own it gives the people who grow a brand no share in what it becomes. Collectibles fix that. Think of the first trading cards of a famous franchise: the bigger the world gets, the more they matter. Our collectibles work the same way for everything built on The Seeker.

  • You help run the world. Holders are the game masters. They're planned to make real-time changes to the game world, place and customize characters on the planet, and steer how it evolves. Today they share a private chat with the team where suggestions shape the game, along with early releases and giveaways.
  • The history is on-chain. We've been inscribing The Seeker's development on Bitcoin since 2023, starting with some of the first ten thousand Ordinals ever made. That timestamped record predates today's AI tools, and every version of The Seeker, whoever builds it, traces back to it.
  • The game itself stays free of monetization. It's open source, playable without owning anything, and never pay-to-win. Collectibles capture the attention the brand earns instead of charging players for it.
  • Design stays honest. Because the game isn't what's being sold, design decisions are made for the game, not for profit. There are no publishers or middlemen in between, and anyone can contribute as much or as little as they want.

Collectibles are art and membership in the community. They aren't an investment product, and nothing here promises financial returns.

Collections

SEEKER NET (Robinhood Chain)

SEEKER NET brings the world to a new chain. It's a complete game and a social, economic, and art experiment of its own: 2,222 fully animated seekers charge toward activation, and the path each one takes becomes its final art.

  • Every seeker below 9,000 charge gains 50 charge a day. At 9,000 it activates.
  • Moving a seeker (a sale, a gift, or a transfer between wallets) releases a quarter of its charge, split evenly among every other seeker still charging. Every move makes everyone else stronger.
  • Discharge is a seeker's latest release, and Uplink is everything it has ever given to the network.
  • There are two quests. The main quest is charge: hold until activation. The side quest is Discharge and Uplink: power the network on the way there. No strategy is best, there are only different paths, and the rarest seekers will be the ones that took the paths fewer people chose.
  • No new NFTs will be minted. Instead, top pixel artists keep adding traits to the existing seekers, until every decision, community participation included, shows up in the art.

The Seeker (Ordinals)

The Seeker is the game's development diary, inscribed as it happens. Many of its first pieces are in the Sub-10K club, among the first ten thousand inscriptions on Bitcoin.

Planetary Primes (Ordinals)

Planetary Primes are strange mathematical artifacts inscribed on prime-numbered sats, a nod to the mathematical layer of the lore. They come with community access and giveaways, and once the game launches, Primes holders are planned to be able to change the game world in real time.

Planetary Guardians (Ordinals)

Planetary Guardians are alien demigods who will appear on the planet. Holders are planned to be able to customize their guardian in the game and choose where it stands. They also make great profile pictures.

Build on it

  1. Clone the repository, or point your AI agent at it.
  2. Get a feel for the world from the design vision, the lore, and the art direction.
  3. Make something, in any medium. Credit The Seeker and share it.

Every creation grows the brand, and the brand belongs to everyone who builds it.
